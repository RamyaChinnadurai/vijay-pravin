38 years in the police: what my father taught me about leadership

Vijay Pravin MaharajanNovember 3, 2026 · 4 min read

In 2026, after 38 years in public service, my father retired from the Tamil Nadu Police.

Over his career, he served as a Driver, Writer, Traffic Constable, Head Constable, Special Sub-Inspector and finally as a Sub-Inspector of Police.

Serving across southern Tamil Nadu meant years of pressure, public scrutiny, violence and human tragedy. These were not easy years. But he never stopped showing up.

Growing up in police quarters

I grew up in police quarters, with a father in uniform and a mother who was a teacher. Money was tight. I remember my parents saving small amounts every month so that one day I could study further. That early lesson in delayed gratification shaped how I think about money, risk and building companies to this day.

Leadership without a title

Watching my father taught me that leadership isn't defined by titles or recognition.

  • It's built through consistency
  • It's strengthened by integrity
  • It's measured by the lives you serve, even when no one is watching

He rarely spoke about sacrifice. He didn't explain every struggle. He simply showed up, again and again, until service became his identity.

Why his story resonated

When I shared his retirement online, it connected with more people than anything I had written in a long time. I think it's because we all know someone like him. Someone quiet. Someone dependable. Someone who never asked for applause.

What I carry into my own work

Trust is not built in one big moment. It's built through years of small, consistent actions. That's true in families, in leadership and in building any company worth building.

Behind every professional milestone, there's usually someone who made quiet sacrifices long before the titles existed. For me, that's Appa and Amma.

Congratulations on your retirement, Appa. You've earned every bit of it.
