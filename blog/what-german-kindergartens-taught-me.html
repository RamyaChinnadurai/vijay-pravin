What German kindergartens taught me about parenting

Vijay Pravin MaharajanNovember 3, 2026 · 4 min read

When I first dropped my daughter at a kindergarten in Germany, I was shocked.

No alphabets on the walls. No number charts. No tiny desks lined up in rows.

Coming from a place where ranks and grades mean everything, this felt almost wrong. Where was the learning?

What she is taught instead

  • How to navigate a shop and place an order
  • How to clean and care for her surroundings
  • How to express what she feels and needs
  • How to play, fall, get up and try again

In Germany, formal schooling with textbooks usually starts around six. Before that, the focus is on independence, social skills and curiosity.

The day she ordered her own food

The moment it really clicked for me was at a café. My daughter, then three, walked up to the counter and ordered her own food. Confidently. In German.

I remember thinking: at her age, I was being drilled on spellings. She was learning to deal with the world.

What I had to unlearn

Growing up in a working-class family in Tamil Nadu, education was the golden ticket. Good marks meant a better future, and there wasn't much room to talk about anything else. I'm grateful for that discipline. It got me to Germany.

But living here for over a decade has shown me another side. Confidence, independence and emotional intelligence are not "extras". They are the foundation everything else is built on.

The best of both worlds

I don't think one system is simply better than the other. India gave me hunger, resilience and respect for hard work. Germany is giving my daughter space to grow at her own pace.

As parents, I think our job is to take the best of both, and to be willing to question what we were taught. Sometimes the best education doesn't look like education at all.
